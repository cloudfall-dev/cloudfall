### Added

- A MariaDB service (S-019) is audited and scraped for metrics. `cloudfall audit` adds six checks per service, none renamed or removed: `mariadb.version` is drift when the installed `mariadb-server` is another release than `version`; `mariadb.series` reports the server as running an unsupported version once its series is past its end of life at the observation's time; `mariadb.unit` is drift when `mariadb.service` is not running and enabled; `mariadb.configuration` is drift when the settings of the managed `/etc/mysql/mariadb.conf.d/90-cloudfall.cnf` differ from what the role writes (a changed, added or removed setting; comments are not compared); `mariadb.role` is drift when a replica is writable (`read_only` off) or its replication threads are not both running, or when a standalone server or a primary replicates from somewhere; and, for a replica only, `mariadb.lag` is drift when it is more than five minutes behind its primary. `inspect` reads the managed file as text and, as root through the socket, `read_only` and `SHOW SLAVE STATUS` (the threads' states, the lag and the source's `server_id`, never a password) into a new `spec.mariadb` of the observation; a record an older inspect wrote has none, so those checks report unknown until the next inspect. With `metrics.enabled`, the role makes the `alloy` account on every server of the service, a replica's too, authenticated by the unix socket only and outside the binary log, with `PROCESS` and `SLAVE MONITOR`; the collector renders one Alloy `prometheus.exporter.mysql` for the service, connecting through the server's socket as that account with no password, labelled with the service id, and none without `metrics.enabled`. The bundled Services dashboard gains MariaDB panels: up, connections, queries and replication lag (#425)
